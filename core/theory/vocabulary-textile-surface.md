# Cross-domain vocabulary: textile and surface

A working solution to "make one flat material carry structure, hierarchy, and identity" that existed
before the web. Use this when a surface has to do work without adding elements.

## What the material already solved

- **Structure from the weave.** Pattern is a by-product of construction, so the surface carries
  information without anything being applied on top of it. The analogue is a layout whose grid is
  legible through the composition rather than drawn over it.
- **Repeat and interruption.** A regular repeat establishes expectation cheaply; a single break in it
  is then enormously legible. This is the most transferable idea in the whole list — one deviation
  reads more strongly than any amount of added emphasis.
- **Selvedge and seam as honest edges.** How a thing is finished is visible and is not hidden. An
  interface that shows how it is put together earns a specific kind of trust.
- **Scale change across one pattern.** The same motif at three scales reads as a family rather than
  three unrelated textures.
- **Material honesty.** Linen looks like linen. A finish that pretends to be another material reads
  as a failure of intent rather than a style.

## What it offers a screen

- Hierarchy through one systematic decision repeated, with a single deliberate break for the moment
  that matters.
- Surfaces that distinguish regions through treatment rather than through added borders and shadows.
- Repeated components that read as a family through scale, not through variation in colour.

## What not to take

Texture as applied decoration. Noise overlays, faux fabric, and grain filters are costume, and they
usually cost legibility in the bargain. The transferable part is systematic repetition and the power
of one interruption.

## Where it misleads

A textile is not read for meaning; a screen is. A pattern that merely pleases can still obscure a
label or reduce contrast below an accessible floor. Repetition earns its place only where it carries
a distinction the reader needs.
